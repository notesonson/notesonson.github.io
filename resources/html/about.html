About

Notes on Son is an experiment on Mexican son jarocho music that expands traditional cruzado and derecho categories to predict jarana strumming patterns, challenging conventional 6/8 meter editions while allowing for respectful and creative recreation of the style.

This site hosts a database of transcriptions, available free of charge for further analysis or to be used as a learning tool.

Technical

Strumming

Transcriptions remove ornaments and split music in bars of 6 eighth notes starting with the singing (favored) or instrumental solo line, making strumming up or down the only options.

The set of all possible strumming patterns or permutations is arranged in the fashion of a 6-bit binary system, and numbered from 1 to 64.

Based on accentuation, permutations are classified as cruzado (3+3/8), derecho (2+2+2/8), both, or neither.

Finally, sones are divided into four categories: pure cruzado, exhibiting only cruzado permutations; pure derecho, exhibiting only derecho permutations; mixed cruzado, exhibiting both cruzado and derecho permutations and off-beat pickup bars; and mixed derecho, exhibiting both cruzado and derecho permutations and on-beat or no pickup bars.

Permutation occurrences will be tracked for later statistical examination.

Limitations

Sones obligados (forced rhythm) or in simple meter (e.g. Bamba) may not be consistent with permutations. They will be marked accordingly.

Mixed sones offer two reasonable, overlapping transcription choices: #22 (2+2+2/8) + #19 (3+3/8) or #10 (3+3/8) + #22 (2+2+2/8). Again, transcriptions will align with the singing or instrumental solo line for consistency.

Regional, familial, and individual variations may contradict each other. Each version will be labeled with the closest matching category.

Furthermore, sones are ornamented during performance. Irregular permutation ocurrences will be ignored.

Tuning

Secondary or unsourced transcriptions (captioned as learned from) use mayor (major) tuning.

Notes are presented from fifth to first course, disregarding register, it being subject to instrument construction and player preference.

Chord diagrams for tonic, subdominant, dominant, and some common passing chords are provided for illustrative purposes but analysis of extended harmony falls beyond the scope of this work.

A tone generator was built to assist tuning.

Recommended reading

For more information on tuning, see Luis Argüello’s The voices of the jarana, a comprehensive work on popular and rare tunings including the beautifully-named alta prima (high first, most excelent), inventada (made-up), la trampa (the trickery), and mañosidad (unorthodox dexterity).

To know more about the intersections between son jarocho and the historically informed performance movement, see Emil Rzajev’s Entre lloronas e ympossibles (in Spanish).

For a verse repository, visit Enrique Morán’s ongoing work Jarochance (in Spanish).
